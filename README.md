# TBA basis regime pipeline

Structural-break and regime detection for a hedged, roll-adjusted TBA basis series, with replication of the quant hedge-ratio methodology, desk-vs-quant diagnostics, and a forecast-evaluation harness. Written to run today on synthetic data with planted breaks (so every detector is validated against known truth) and to accept your real data later via a documented CSV schema.

Operational guide for producing inputs from a production intraday stack and reading the outputs: see `USAGE.md`. Run the smoke test with `python run.py --synthetic`; run on real data with `python run.py --data-dir ./data`. Install dependencies with `pip install -r requirements.txt`. All outputs (CSVs, figures, `report.md`) land in `./outputs`.

## Data specification

Place these CSVs in one directory. Dates as YYYY-MM-DD; prices in decimal points (convert from 32nds upstream); rates and coupons in percent.

**Required.** `tba_prices.csv`: columns `date, coupon, price_front, price_back` — front and back settlement month closes per coupon (long format). `roll_dates.csv`: `roll_date` — the LAST day each outgoing month is the front (labels advance the next day; confirm your vendor's switch-day convention matches). Treasury leg, either form: `treasury.csv` (`date, tsy_price, tsy_yield`) if you already maintain a continuous back-adjusted series; or `treasury_raw.csv` (`date, ct10_price, wi_price[, ct10_yield]`) plus `tsy_roll_dates.csv` (`tsy_roll_date` — the last day each outgoing note is the hedge), and the pipeline splices held-instrument changes itself: on the day after a switch the change is CT10_new(t) − WI(t−1), the same CUSIP priced yesterday as when-issued, so no coupon/maturity level artifact enters. The WI price is only needed on the days bracketing each switch. The CT10−WI spread is saved as a liquidity/flight-to-quality covariate.

**Optional but valuable.** `current_coupon.csv` (`date, cc`): supply your production CC series; otherwise the pipeline computes a par coupon and flags extrapolation days. `hedge_ratio_desk.csv` (`date, coupon, ratio`): the desk's actual ratios, as-of end of day. `vol.csv` (`date, vol`): swaption vol, one consistent convention (e.g. 3m10y normal). `repo.csv` (`date, repo`): for the specialness proxy. `hedge_ratio_quant.csv` (`date, coupon, ratio`): the PRODUCTION quant ratios if exportable — the pipeline horse-races them against both replication variants, which settles empirically whether production runs the literal (A) or fixed-instrument (B) procedure and therefore whether the composition-effect critique applies to it. `events.csv` (`date, label`): candidate macro dates — FOMC meetings, tariff announcements, geopolitical shocks — for the event overlay in the drift module.

The synthetic generator writes this exact schema to `outputs/synthetic_data/`, so you have a working template to match.

## Module map

`config.py` holds every methodological knob. `synth.py` generates the planted-break sample. `hedge.py` replicates the quant hedge-ratio pipeline in two variants. `basis.py` builds roll-adjusted changes, the hedged basis, and desk diagnostics. `regimes.py` is the break/regime battery. `drift.py` answers the carry-vs-widening drift question. `forecast.py` is the walk-forward evaluation harness. `run.py` orchestrates and writes the report.

## The central methodological point: Variant A vs Variant B

Your step 2 regresses *changes in the interpolated prices at each moneyness node*. Because the node is anchored to the current coupon and the current coupon moves with rates, differencing that series measures the sensitivity of a portfolio that continuously rebalances along the coupon stack to stay at constant moneyness — not the sensitivity of the fixed coupon a book actually holds. Sliding along the stack offsets part of the rate move (the offset is roughly ∂P/∂coupon × dCC/dy, which can be a large fraction of duration), so the literal procedure systematically understates the hedge for a held coupon, and understates it more when the current coupon is mobile.

The pipeline therefore implements both: **Variant A** (literal — difference the moneyness-anchored series) and **Variant B** (fixed-instrument — difference each actual coupon's roll-adjusted price first, then interpolate the *changes* to nodes anchored at yesterday's CC). It reports the A−B gap at the CC node and its correlation with CC mobility. In the synthetic sample (deliberately exaggerated), A ≈ 0.34 vs B ≈ 1.01 with gap-vs-CC-activity correlation ≈ 0.54; on real data expect a smaller but material gap. Two practical implications: if the quants truly run Variant A, the desk's instinct to run higher ratios after selloffs may be *correcting* the composition effect rather than deviating from best practice; and any regime test run on an A-hedged basis inherits leaked duration that will masquerade as basis regimes. The basis built here uses Variant B ratios, lagged one day.

## Other methodology decisions worth knowing

**Point-in-time discipline.** The hedge applied over day t uses the ratio known at the close of t−1; rolling windows are trailing-only; forecast training pairs are purged with an embargo so no target overlaps a prediction window; HMM parameters are fit on the first 60% of the sample only and then *filtered* forward (smoothed probabilities condition on the future and are not tradeable — they appear in the report for description only).

**Roll handling.** Daily changes are computed on the contract actually held over each interval, so the drop never appears as a spurious jump; the drop is kept as its own column and turned into a specialness proxy against repo, because it is a funding signal rather than noise.

**Current coupon convention risk.** When the whole stack trades away from par (post-2022), a computed par coupon is an extrapolation beyond the top liquid coupon; the pipeline flags those days. If your production CC switched convention historically, that switch will manufacture a break — supply the production series and check the flag column.

**Overlap in rolling betas.** A 60-day rolling beta shares 59/60 observations day to day; naive break tests on it wildly over-reject. Hence: PELT minimum segment ≥ the window, block-bootstrap p-values for sup-F and Nyblom, and the Kalman filter as the preferred detector — its standardized one-step innovations are serially uncorrelated under the null, so CUSUM machinery applies cleanly to them.

**Detector division of labor.** Recursive CUSUM on the hedge regression has low power against pure slope breaks when the regressor is mean-zero (a rate-change regressor is) — expect it to read "never" even across a real duration break; that is a property, not a failure. sup-F is a *test* with a coarse locator under heteroskedastic regressors; localization is the job of PELT and of the Kalman innovation diagnostics (Shewhart flags and the ICSS-style variance locator). In the synthetic run the Kalman Shewhart flags hit the planted duration break to the exact day.

**Hedge-lag artifact check.** After a genuine duration break, the 60-day rolling ratio takes weeks to adapt, and the basis leaks rate beta over that window — which then registers as a basis "regime." The report cross-references basis-break dates against beta-break dates and labels breaks that occur just after a beta break as likely hedge-adaptation artifacts. This is the concrete form of the warning that hedge misspecification masquerades as regime dependence.

**Episode honesty.** The report counts detected episodes (hysteresis regime entries plus break segments) and states that regime inference rests on that count, not on the number of daily rows. With post-crisis data expect single-digit-to-low-double-digit effective episodes; treat marginal p-values accordingly.

**Forecast discipline.** The target is the next-h-day cumulative *hedged* basis return — already the excess-return object. Clark–West handles the nested comparison against the expanding mean (Diebold–Mariano is invalid there); Giacomini–White with a lagged regime instrument answers the question a regime signal actually poses — not "does the model beat the mean on average" but "does the regime predict *when* it does." Economic evaluation is sign-based with turnover costs, since regime signals whipsaw. Do not read the synthetic run's Sharpe ratios as meaningful; the generator has drift by construction and exists only to validate mechanics.

## Drift regimes: the carry-vs-widening question

The cumulative hedged basis normally slopes upward (carry) and occasionally slopes downward (widening episodes -- hiking cycles, risk-off shocks, demand withdrawal). This is a *drift* regime in the P&L itself, and it needs different machinery from the parameter-stability battery, for two statistical reasons.

First, drift regimes are low-SNR objects: with daily basis vol several times the drift differential between the carry and widening states, return-only detection needs on the order of (sigma/delta-mu)^2 trading days of evidence -- realistically two to four weeks -- while vol regime shifts flag in days. So covariates (swaption vol, funding, the spread level itself) are the fast trigger; the drift statistics are the confirmation layer. Slow QT-style widening with no vol spike is the genuinely hard case and is mostly identified ex post by trend segmentation. Second, permanent variance eras hijack naive regime models: a Markov-switching model fit to raw returns allocates its states to vol eras and never sees the mean. The module therefore removes the carry proxy (amortized roll income, ex ante) and studentizes by trailing volatility before fitting the Hamilton MS-mean model, so the states are forced to describe the *slope*. Filtered probabilities only. A PELT trend segmentation provides the dated slope table (mean-shift on returns is mathematically piecewise-linear slopes on the level), labelling each segment carry / flat / widening.

Each widening episode is then attributed across point-in-time candidate explanations: **carry** (roll income), **hedge leakage** (Kalman-filtered beta minus the applied production ratio, times the Treasury move -- the misspecification channel that masquerades as widening), **vol loading** (trailing beta to dVol times the vol move), **convexity** (0.5 x dBeta/dy x dy x dP_tsy -- the realized-variance bleed of a delta-hedged short-convexity position, computed from the fitted hedge-curve slope), and a **residual demand** term, which is the pure spread/demand shock -- the part that is "really" a basis regime. These components overlap by construction (leakage and convexity both touch duration error), so read them as candidate explanations rather than an orthogonal decomposition. Rally/selloff beta asymmetry is estimated per episode as the flight-to-quality signature. When segmentation windows are wide, attribution mixes the episode with its surroundings; for known candidate dates (FOMC, tariff announcements, war headlines) the `events.csv` overlay is the sharper tool -- it reports basis drift and the jump in filtered widening probability around each supplied date.

One insight from building the synthetic validation is worth internalizing: a hedged basis held at a moving moneyness anchor bleeds 0.5 x Gamma x (dCC)^2 *every day* through negative convexity -- in the generator this produced a persistent downward drift before any carry compensation existed, and on real data it means the "normal upward slope" is carry *net of* realized-variance bleed. A calm-but-high-realized-vol stretch can flatten the basis with no spread widening at all; the attribution separates that case from true demand shocks.

## Desk diagnostics

With `hedge_ratio_desk.csv` supplied, the pipeline quantifies the reactive updating you described: the cross-correlation of update magnitude with lagged rate moves (reactive desks show mass at lag ≥ 0, none negative), the update-trigger profile P(update | net move since last update > x) — which on the synthetic sample recovers the planted 15bp trigger almost exactly and on real data reverse-engineers the desk's implicit rule — the desk-minus-quant ratio spread with PELT breaks on it (discretion itself treated as a regime), and the slippage PnL between desk-hedged and quant-hedged books, which prices the deviation.

## Validation checklist when real data arrives

Confirm timestamp alignment between TBA closes, the Treasury leg, and vol (a 3pm/4pm mismatch manufactures lead-lag betas). Confirm both roll calendars use the last-day-of-outgoing-instrument convention. Expect small hedge-ratio steps at on-the-run switches even with a perfect splice — the new note's DV01 differs, so the price-on-price ratio steps for hedge-leg reasons; the report flags beta breaks landing within five days of a Treasury roll as candidate DV01 steps rather than TBA regimes. Confirm 32nds-to-decimal conversion and that the Treasury series is continuous across its own rolls. Check the vol series for a normal/lognormal splice date. Verify the CC source and inspect the extrapolation flag around 2022. Ask how desk ratios are timestamped (before or after the day's rebalance — this changes the slippage attribution). Check coupon liquidity masking against the interpolation coverage diagnostics, and re-run with `curve_fit: spline` to confirm ratio read-offs are not poly3 artifacts near the grid edge. Then compare the A/B gap against the desk-vs-quant spread: if the desk's deviations correlate with the gap, you have your explanation for the deviations.

## Synthetic validation status

All seven planted events are recovered by the intended detectors: the duration break by PELT on the beta path, by sup-F rejection, and by the Kalman Shewhart flags to the day; the basis distribution/variance break by the PELT pair; the vol-link onset by the rolling vol-beta tests; and both planted drift windows (a clean stress episode and a harder one embedded inside a high-vol era) by the MS filtered probabilities and the trend segmentation, with the attribution correctly assigning the first to residual demand and the second largely to convexity bleed. The recovery table prints at the end of `outputs/report.md` on every synthetic run, so any future modification that breaks a detector fails visibly.
